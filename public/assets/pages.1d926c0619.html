About

Creative by trade. Commercial by nature.

I'm Lorcán. Most people call me Lor.

I grew up in Dublin and spent more than eight years living and working in the Netherlands, across Dutch and international organisations, agencies and brands. Most of my career has been built between the two countries.

It started in design and creative direction, and over more than a decade has widened into communications, business development and technology. The disciplines changed; the work underneath didn't. Understand what people actually need, then shape the idea, the message and the product around it.

Today I work on the commercial side of Arkintel, a Dutch technology company working in AI. Alongside that I build my own digital products and tools, and keep a studio of independent brand work.

Working across both sides has taught me that good creative work and good business are rarely in conflict. The best results usually come from people who can hold the idea and the commercial reality in the same conversation.

Outside of work I've written eight books, made music videos and paint when there's time. Less side projects than the same curiosity pointed somewhere else.

If what you're working on could use this mix of creative and commercial thinking, I'd be glad to hear about it.

01 · Maritime robotics · Independent work

Eyes on the water line.

A fictional Dutch engineering company whose autonomous robots inspect dikes, flood defences and the water infrastructure that keeps a low country dry.

WADDEN is built to be trusted by people who inspect dikes for a living. The identity borrows from engineering drawings and safety signage: a navy field, a single warning yellow, mono type for anything measured. Photography stays documentary, shot in the flat light of the Wadden coast, so the robot reads as working equipment rather than a gadget.

Industry
Maritime robotics
Audience
Water boards, infrastructure operators, engineering firms and municipalities.
Status
Self-initiated, fictional brand
Disciplines
Identity · Campaign · Print · Social · Web
Palette

Photography

Documentary, never staged. Flat North Sea light, wet basalt and real weather, because the people buying this inspect dikes for a living and can spot a set-up shot instantly.

Identity

The W reads as two folds of water running down a dike. Yellow means caution on a dike, so it is rationed: the flag in the logo, the bumpers on the machine and very little else.

Print

Brochure and stationery share one grid and one voice: plain engineering language, big photography, specification in mono. Documents that will be photocopied, filed and read on site.

Web

The site behaves like a control room: a live inspection feed, the run explained in three steps and a cross-section of the dike that shows what each sensor finds. It sells the outcome, an engineer who knows the state of every metre.

02 · Small-business software · Independent work

Less administration. More room to run your business.

Fictional software that takes invoices, payment reminders, follow-ups and everyday admin off the people running small businesses, without adding another thing to learn.

Kindling had to feel like a helpful colleague, not another dashboard. The brand pairs a confident sans with an italic serif for the human aside, and speaks in the words a tradesperson would actually send. Photography shows the end of a working day, warm and unposed, so the product is always about the person it gives time back to.

Industry
Small-business software
Audience
Freelancers, tradespeople, café owners and independent professionals.
Status
Self-initiated, fictional brand
Disciplines
Identity · Editorial · Advertising · Product UI · Web
Palette

Voice

Small-business owners don't want software, they want their evenings back. So the brand shows real people at the end of a working day and the actual words a reminder would say, never a floating dashboard.

Identity

A bold sans for the plain facts and an italic serif for the human aside. Purple and cream are the paper; green only ever means something got done.

Editorial

The newsletter reads like a small magazine rather than a product update: one lead story, a line to copy, three things to do before Friday. Its readers owe it nothing, so it has to earn the open.

Web

A masthead, a big headline and a collage of real moments. The website is set like the newsletter, so the brand feels the same wherever you meet it.

03 · Premium audio · Independent work

Sound, without compromise.

A fictional premium audio company making wireless headphones and home speakers that feel considered, tactile and effortless, and desirable before they are switched on.

RUNE is about restraint. Charcoal, off-white and real metal carry the brand, and violet is allowed once per layout, the way the light ring sits on the headphones. Every image is lit with one hard source on stone, plaster or fabric, so materials do the persuading and the typography stays quiet.

Industry
Premium audio
Audience
Music lovers, designers and people who care how their things are made.
Status
Self-initiated, fictional brand
Disciplines
Identity · Product imagery · Packaging · Outdoor · Web
Palette

Campaign

The object first, then the craft: the headphones on stone in one hard light, then the machined yoke up close. The type stays quiet so the product is the loudest thing in the frame.

Identity

Charcoal, off-white and real metal. Violet appears once per layout, the way the light ring sits on the headphones, and nowhere else.

Editorial

The brand earns its place in a magazine by being about listening, not specifications. A concrete room, a slice of winter sun and one record played start to finish.

04 · Modular furniture · Independent work

Build the room. Rebuild it later.

A fictional Dutch furniture company making modular pieces for modern homes: sofas, shelving and blocks that grow, shrink and move house with you.

FOLD sells a system, not a sofa. The logo is drawn from the same folded blocks as the furniture, and the language is about planning a room rather than buying a product. The photography lives in real Dutch rooms with low sun and lived-in props, and terracotta carries from the bouclé through to the box tape.

Industry
Modular furniture
Audience
Design-conscious renters and homeowners in city apartments.
Status
Self-initiated, fictional brand
Disciplines
Identity · Catalogue · Retail · Packaging · E-commerce
Palette

Catalogue

Every sofa starts as a single seat. The catalogue sells the system rather than the sofa, so the language is about planning a room in blocks.

Retail and packaging

Terracotta and forest green carry from the bouclé to the box tape. The flat-pack boxes are numbered like the modules inside, so delivery day is part of the brand.

Identity

The logo is built the way the furniture is: four letters made of folded, interlocking blocks.

Web

An architectural catalogue: an index down one side, one room at a time, and every piece shown as part of a set you can configure.
